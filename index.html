MAVREC scene 1: Stage 1 re-rendering

What we did

We are building Stage 1 of a system that edits how an object moves through a drone video. To train it, we need pairs of videos: the same scene shot from two different camera angles, with the tracked object's box known in both. Since we only have one real camera angle per scene, we generate the second angle synthetically, using models that can re-render an existing video as if it were filmed from a different camera position. Video 1 is the real, original footage all of the tests below start from.

Our first choice was ReCamMaster, a video generation model built for exactly this task. Video 2 shows its output when we rotate the camera by about 20 degrees. The render looks plausible at first, but by the later frames it invents a building in the corner of the frame that does not exist in the real scene. We thought this might be because the rotation was too large, so we tried again with half the rotation in Video 3. That did not fix it either. This time it invented a different structure and changed the sky. Since the model was not asked to imagine a building or a different sky, we treat this as ReCamMaster guessing at content it was never shown, rather than a setting we can tune away.

Rather than stop at that finding, we carried the ReCamMaster output through the rest of the pipeline to see what the downstream steps look like. Video 6 shows point tracks placed on top of Video 2, following how points on the object and background move frame to frame. Video 7 shows the tracked object's box relocated on Video 2 using those tracks, so we can see where the system would place the object if this render were used as training data.

We then tried TrajectoryCrafter, which works differently. Instead of asking a model to draw the whole new frame from scratch, it estimates the depth of the real scene and moves the existing pixels to where they should appear from the new camera position. A model only fills in the small parts of the frame that are genuinely new and were never visible in the original shot. Video 4 repeats the same 10 degree rotation as Video 3, this time with no invented buildings or sky. Video 5 tries a larger, combined tilt and pan, to check that the result holds up on a bigger change, and it does. Video 8 goes further and moves the camera forward, which is a harder test because it requires real depth, not just rotation. Nearby objects like the sign board grow and shift while distant trees barely move, which is what a real forward move should look like, and again there is nothing invented.
