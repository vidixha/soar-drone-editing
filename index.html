MAVREC scene 1: Stage 1 re-rendering

This page is part of a replication of the TRACE paper. Stage 1 of TRACE, applied here to the MAVREC drone dataset, requires pairs of videos showing the same scene from different camera viewpoints, together with the tracked object's bounding box in both views. Since each real scene is recorded from only a single viewpoint, we synthesize a second viewpoint using camera-controlled video generation models. Video 1 is the original drone footage used as input for all experiments below.

We first evaluated ReCamMaster, a model designed for camera-controlled video re-rendering. Video 2 shows its output after rotating the camera by approximately 20°. Although the rendered video appears realistic initially, later frames contain a building in the upper-right corner that is absent from the original footage. To determine whether this was caused by the magnitude of the viewpoint change, we repeated the experiment with a smaller 10° rotation (Video 3). The artifacts remained, with the model introducing a new structure near the horizon and altering the appearance of the sky. Since these regions are not visible in the original video, the generated content cannot be verified from the input observations.

To understand how these artifacts would affect later stages of the pipeline, we continued processing the ReCamMaster output. Video 6 shows CoTracker point tracks over the rendered video, and Video 7 shows the tracked object's bounding box transferred using those tracks. This illustrates how Stage 2 would operate if Stage 1 re-rendering were generated by ReCamMaster.

We then evaluated TrajectoryCrafter, which follows a different approach. Rather than synthesizing every frame directly, it first estimates scene geometry and projects pixels from the original video into the target viewpoint, using a generative model only to complete regions that become newly visible after reprojection.

Video 4 repeats the same 10° rotation used in Video 3. Unlike ReCamMaster, the rendered scene preserves the appearance of the sky and surrounding structures. Video 5 applies a larger combined pan and tilt to evaluate a more challenging camera motion, while Video 8 evaluates forward camera translation. In Video 8, nearby objects such as the roadside sign exhibit larger apparent motion than distant trees, consistent with the depth-dependent parallax expected from forward camera movement.
