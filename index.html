MAVREC scene 1: Stage 1 re-rendering — ReCamMaster vs. TrajectoryCrafter

What we did

Stage 1 of the TRACE replication needs to re-render a real drone clip from a new camera angle, so a small model can later learn how an object's tracked box should move when the camera changes. Part 1 below is our first attempt, ReCamMaster (a pretrained video diffusion model). Part 2 is what we switched to after ReCamMaster's problem showed up: TrajectoryCrafter.

Problem (Part 1): ReCamMaster hallucinated fictional content in the region newly revealed by the camera rotation — a fake building appeared for a ~20° rotation, and a different, arguably worse glowing fake structure (plus an invented overcast sky) appeared even after halving the rotation to ~10°. Halving the angle did not fix it, pointing to a domain gap: these models are trained on ground-level video, not top-down aerial footage.

Fix (Part 2): TrajectoryCrafter is a hybrid approach that estimates real depth (via DepthCrafter) and reprojects the actual scene geometry via a point-cloud warp, using diffusion only to fill in the small genuinely-unknown occlusion holes — not to hallucinate the whole frame. Result: no fabricated structures across three different trajectories we tested (rotation, a combined tilt+pan, and a forward dolly with genuine 3D parallax), and each render was faster and cheaper than ReCamMaster (~5 min / ~$0.30 vs ~33 min / ~$0.44 per trajectory).

Getting TrajectoryCrafter running took real debugging, all logged in modal/DEBUG_LOG.md: an unfiltered checkpoint download that pulled ~98GB instead of ~30GB, a torchvision version silently clobbered by a transitive dependency (twice — same pattern as an earlier huggingface_hub incident on the ReCamMaster pipeline), a missing torch API that a sitecustomize.py shim couldn't fix because Modal's own runtime shadows it, an xformers wheel built for a completely different torch/CUDA version, and a hard 49-frame limit on the CogVideoX-Fun-V1.1-5b backbone (couldn't extend to our source clip's full 81 frames). Each was caught and fixed on CPU-only debug functions before spending on the A100, keeping wasted GPU spend to a few minutes total across all the failed attempts.

Part 2 — TrajectoryCrafter

Three trajectories tested: a mild rotation, a more dynamic combined tilt+pan, and a forward dolly (the hardest case — real 3D parallax, not just rotation). All three came out clean, with no fabricated structures.

Video 4 — TrajectoryCrafter, theta=10° (depth warp + diffusion inpainting)

No hallucinated buildings or fabricated structures across all 49 frames — scene stays geometrically/semantically coherent. Only minor artifact: the sign's text looks slightly different. 5.2 min render, ~$0.30-0.35 on A100 (cheaper than ReCamMaster despite the pricier GPU, since diffusion only fills small occlusion holes, not the whole frame).

Video 8 — TrajectoryCrafter, forward dolly (r=0.3, no rotation)

Tests real forward translation, not just rotation — the harder case (TrajectoryCrafter's own follow-up critique paper, DepthDirector, specifically flags "flying pixels" and geometric distortion under translation as its main weakness). Confirmed genuine 3D parallax, not a 2D zoom: the sign board (tall, near) grows and shifts noticeably while the distant treeline barely changes — a uniform zoom would scale both equally. No distortion or flying pixels across all 49 frames. 5.1 min render, ~$0.30.
